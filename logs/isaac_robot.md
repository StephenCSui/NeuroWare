## Session 8 — NeuroWare (2026-08-11)

### Current state
Working: single-robot autonomous drive-to-shelf-and-lift (`pickup_demo.py`, P-triggered), and full two-robot cooperative lift-turn-carry-place (`two_robot_pickup_demo.py`, P-triggered) — both verified end to end with correct final positions and the carried object staying on the plates throughout. Piston force, ball-joint (roll/pitch/yaw) force, and turning are all tuned and hold up under real load.

### What was done
- **Piston position-hold ("floating")**: switched the piston from velocity/damping-only drive to real position control (`PISTON_STIFFNESS=20000`, `PISTON_DAMPING=1000`), with an accumulated `piston_target` updated by measured `dt` on R/C key hold. Damping-only can't resist a constant load (gravity) — only stiffness (real position control) holds a static height under weight.
- **Chassis-height / piston-reach fix**: root-caused why the platform couldn't reach far enough down to slide under shelf objects. A link's own `xformOp:translate` in USD only affects the pre-simulation rest pose — the actual simulated kinematic offset between articulated bodies is driven by the connecting **joint's** `physics:localPos0/1` in `physics.usda`. Fixed `centered_piston_prismatic_z`'s `localPos0` (0.065→0.01) and `rod_to_ball_fixed`'s `localPos0` (0.125→0.02). Closed a 22.1cm gap down to 6.1cm (residual gap is the actuator's own fixed 0.1m chassis mount height, deliberately left alone).
- **Turning stall fix**: after an earlier chassis mass increase (5kg→40kg base_link, ~280:1 ratio vs ~0.14kg wheel links), wheels commanded to turn sat at literally 0 velocity regardless of drive torque (confirmed: 5x more damping made zero difference — ruled out as a torque problem). Root cause: PhysX solver couldn't converge the constraint across that mass ratio with default iteration counts. Fixed via `PhysxArticulationAPI.SolverPositionIterationCount=255`, `SolverVelocityIterationCount=64` on the articulation root (`base_link`) — not by touching drive torque.
- **Piston force cap**: `centered_piston_prismatic_z`'s `drive:linear:physics:maxForce` was 100N (real actuator spec) — far below what's needed to lift real payload masses (16kg needs ~157N just statically). Raised progressively (100→1000→130→300→200) via live empirical testing, since **static weight×gravity math consistently underestimated the real dynamic force needed** — a value that should have worked by the formula left objects barely liftable in practice. Final calibrated value: **200N**, chosen specifically so 3 of 4 shelf-1 payload objects (2-13.44kg range) lift solo and the heaviest (Cube_01, tuned to 13.44-18kg at various points) requires two robots.
- **Leftover collision-cylinder jam**: after the joint-offset fix shrank the mechanism, `piston_rod_link`'s own collision proxy (`cylinder_1`, still sized for the old longer geometry — 0.11m tall) was 2cm into the shelf's front support panel, causing wheel-velocity chaos the instant the robot's XY crossed under the shelf. Shrunk to 0.02m (matching the new compact joint span) in `base.usda`. This was a genuine leftover from an earlier visual-mesh cleanup that never touched the paired collision mesh.
- **Ball-joint stack real position control**: roll, pitch, and yaw were all originally damping-only (`stiffness=0`), which can slow a moving joint but can't resist a sustained torque (the plate tilting under an off-center/heavy object's weight). Gave all three real position control (`stiffness=5000, damping=200`) and raised their `maxForce` from 20→200 each (yaw was already fixed at 200 from an earlier pass — a `replace_all` edit accidentally corrupted it to 2000 via an unintended "20" substring match inside "200"; caught and fixed).
- **Active yaw compensation**: the ball-joint yaw DOF actively counters the chassis's own turning (`apply_yaw_compensation` — reads current chassis yaw vs. a recorded reference, commands `yaw_base - delta` on the yaw joint every tick) so the plate's world-frame orientation stays constant while the chassis rotates underneath it. Without this, the plate got rigidly dragged around with the chassis during any turn, putting torque straight into whatever was resting on it.
- **Spawn orientation, corrected twice**: first attempt faced the robot at the shelf's long side (2.16m face) instead of its short side (0.58m face) — the contact plate is a rectangle (0.105×0.08 local), and approaching via the long side puts the plate's long axis perpendicular to the shelf's, jamming it against the (now-removed) front support panel. Fixed by entering from a short side instead, keeping the plate's long axis parallel to the shelf's.
- **Two-robot cooperative carry** (`two_robot_pickup_demo.py`): both robots enter from *opposite* short sides (Robot facing -X, Robot2 facing +X) to straddle the payload object symmetrically. Sequence: drive-in (independent stop-on-X-threshold) → lift together (synchronized piston ramp) → Robot turns to face delivery direction (Robot2 frozen) → Robot2 turns (Robot frozen) → both drive straight to the delivery Y. Turning is staggered (not simultaneous) specifically to avoid the two chassis colliding — they start nose-to-nose with very little clearance.
- **Turn-to-exact-angle, not bearing-to-point**: earlier version computed a live bearing (`atan2`) to an absolute (x,y) target every tick, with continuous gentle steering correction during the drive. This was **rejected by the user** as unreliable — any small residual heading error meant the "straight" drive wasn't actually straight, so the two robots didn't stay predictably positioned relative to each other. Replaced with: turn to an *exact known* absolute yaw (analytically known from the fixed pickup headings, not derived from position), then drive with **equal wheel speeds and zero steering correction** once that heading is locked in. This fully fixed the correctness of the final position.
- **Stop-condition tolerance bug**: `drive_straight_step`'s stop condition was `abs(target_y - y) < stop_dist`. Since travel is monotonic (always approaching from below), this triggers the instant the robot enters that band from below — i.e. at `target_y - stop_dist`, not at `target_y`. Every prior "closest approach" (3.10 with stop_dist=0.15, then 3.206 with stop_dist=0.05) was landing systematically short by ~stop_dist, not from motor overshoot or the wrong target as first suspected. Fixed by checking `y >= target_y - small_margin` (reached-or-passed) instead of a symmetric distance band. See `feedback_small_scale_tight_tolerances` memory.
- **Speed ramping**: an instant full-speed (5.0) command from a standstill was what actually knocked the unattached payload object loose during the drive phase — not gradual path divergence, which was the first (wrong) hypothesis. Both `turn_to_yaw_step` and `drive_straight_step` now ramp speed linearly over `RAMP_STEPS=90` (~1.5s) from a standstill.
- **stdout buffering bug**: none of the script's `print()` output was reaching the redirected log file — Python fully block-buffers stdout when piped to a file (unlike a terminal), and the buffer was never filling/flushing while the process stayed alive. Fixed by launching with `PYTHONUNBUFFERED=1`.

### What worked
- Position-control (stiffness, not just damping) is the correct fix pattern for every "joint doesn't hold under load" symptom seen this session — piston, then later roll/pitch/yaw. Damping alone only ever resists *motion*, never a static load.
- Empirical, direct-measurement force tuning (drive it, measure whether the object actually lifts, adjust) was consistently more trustworthy than the static weight×gravity formula, which undersold real requirements every time it was tried.
- Exact-known-angle-then-straight-line was categorically more robust than continuous bearing-based correction for keeping two independently-controlled robots predictably positioned relative to each other.

### What did not work
- Continuous bearing-based steering correction during the drive phase (`atan2` toward a live target point each tick) — technically converges to the right point in isolation, but doesn't guarantee straight-line travel, which is what actually matters when a second robot's position needs to stay predictable relative to the first. Replaced with the exact-angle approach above.
- First hypothesis for the object falling off during carry: gradual heading-error divergence over the ~4m drive. Actually wrong — direct headless testing showed the drop happens almost immediately (within ~3 seconds of starting to move), which pointed to the real cause (instant full-speed command from a standstill), not slow drift.
- First hypothesis for the "wrong stop location": assumed rigid-coupling-mechanism absence (object sliding around) was the root cause of everything, including position. Wrong — position was a pure control-logic bug (the stop-condition tolerance issue above), unrelated to whether the object stays attached.

### Key decisions
- **No rigid coupling mechanism built yet** (the originally-planned "phase-one" fixed joint between plate and object). The tolerance/speed-ramp/exact-angle fixes above turned out to be sufficient to keep the object on the plates through a full pickup→turn→carry→arrive cycle via careful motion control alone, so building an explicit joint attachment was deprioritized. Still likely needed for a more general case (e.g. turning en route, not just at fixed points, or a bumpier path).
- **Piston/ball-joint maxForce values are testing-phase, not hardware-accurate** — deliberately raised past real actuator specs (same pattern as the earlier travel-limit override) so mechanism behavior can be tuned/observed without being bottlenecked by real hardware force ratings.
