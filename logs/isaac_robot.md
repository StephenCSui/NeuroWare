# Isaac Sim Robot — Component Log

Detailed history of the reference robot (`gitignore/paper_robot_centered_actuator_v6/...`) and its control code (`isaac/robot_smoke_test.py`, `isaac/pickup_demo.py`, `isaac/two_robot_pickup_demo.py`): joint geometry, physics tuning, drive/lift/turn control logic. Environment/scene-side history (shelf, payload objects, world layout) is in `isaac_environment.md`.

## Session 8 — NeuroWare (2026-08-11)

### Current state
Working: single-robot autonomous drive-to-shelf-and-lift (`pickup_demo.py`, P-triggered), and full two-robot cooperative lift-turn-carry-place (`two_robot_pickup_demo.py`, P-triggered) — both verified end to end with correct final positions and the carried object staying on the plates throughout. Piston force, ball-joint (roll/pitch/yaw) force, and turning are all tuned and hold up under real load.

### What was done
- **Piston position-hold ("floating")**: switched the piston from velocity/damping-only drive to real position control (`PISTON_STIFFNESS=20000`, `PISTON_DAMPING=1000`), with an accumulated `piston_target` updated by measured `dt` on R/C key hold. Damping-only can't resist a constant load (gravity) — only stiffness (real position control) holds a static height under weight.
- **Chassis-height / piston-reach fix**: root-caused why the platform couldn't reach far enough down to slide under shelf objects. A link's own `xformOp:translate` in USD only affects the pre-simulation rest pose — the actual simulated kinematic offset between articulated bodies is driven by the connecting **joint's** `physics:localPos0/1` in `physics.usda`. Fixed `centered_piston_prismatic_z`'s `localPos0` (0.065→0.01) and `rod_to_ball_fixed`'s `localPos0` (0.125→0.02). Closed a 22.1cm gap down to 6.1cm (residual gap is the actuator's own fixed 0.1m chassis mount height, deliberately left alone).
- **Turning stall fix**: after an earlier chassis mass increase (5kg→40kg base_link, ~280:1 ratio vs ~0.14kg wheel links), wheels commanded to turn sat at literally 0 velocity regardless of drive torque (confirmed: 5x more damping made zero difference — ruled out as a torque problem). Root cause: PhysX solver couldn't converge the constraint across that mass ratio with default iteration counts. Fixed via `PhysxArticulationAPI.SolverPositionIterationCount=255`, `SolverVelocityIterationCount=64` on the articulation root (`base_link`) — not by touching drive torque.
- **Piston force cap**: `centered_piston_prismatic_z`'s `drive:linear:physics:maxForce` was 100N (real actuator spec) — far below what's needed to lift real payload masses (16kg needs ~157N just statically). Raised progressively (100→1000→130→300→200) via live empirical testing, since **static weight×gravity math consistently underestimated the real dynamic force needed** — a value that should have worked by the formula left objects barely liftable in practice. Final calibrated value: **200N**, chosen specifically so 3 of 4 shelf-1 payload objects (2-13.44kg range) lift solo and the heaviest (Cube_01, tuned to 13.44-18kg at various points) requires two robots.
- **Leftover collision-cylinder jam**: after the joint-offset fix shrank the mechanism, `piston_rod_link`'s own collision proxy (`cylinder_1`, still sized for the old longer geometry — 0.11m tall) was 2cm into the shelf's front support panel, causing wheel-velocity chaos the instant the robot's XY crossed under the shelf. Shrunk to 0.02m (matching the new compact joint span) in `base.usda`. This was a genuine leftover from an earlier visual-mesh cleanup that never touched the paired collision mesh.
- **Ball-joint stack real position control**: roll, pitch, and yaw were all originally damping-only (`stiffness=0`), which can slow a moving joint but can't resist a sustained torque (the plate tilting under an off-center/heavy object's weight). Gave all three real position control (`stiffness=5000, damping=200`) and raised their `maxForce` from 20→200 each (yaw was already fixed at 200 from an earlier pass — a `replace_all` edit accidentally corrupted it to 2000 via an unintended "20" substring match inside "200"; caught and fixed).
- **Active yaw compensation**: the ball-joint yaw DOF actively counters the chassis's own turning (`apply_yaw_compensation` — reads current chassis yaw vs. a recorded reference, commands `yaw_base - delta` on the yaw joint every tick) so the plate's world-frame orientation stays constant while the chassis rotates underneath it. Without this, the plate got rigidly dragged around with the chassis during any turn, putting torque straight into whatever was resting on it.
- **Spawn orientation, corrected twice**: first attempt faced the robot at the shelf's long side (2.16m face) instead of its short side (0.58m face) — the contact plate is a rectangle (0.105×0.08 local), and approaching via the long side puts the plate's long axis perpendicular to the shelf's, jamming it against the (now-removed) front support panel. Fixed by entering from a short side instead, keeping the plate's long axis parallel to the shelf's.
- **Two-robot cooperative carry** (`two_robot_pickup_demo.py`): both robots enter from *opposite* short sides (Robot facing -X, Robot2 facing +X) to straddle the payload object symmetrically. Sequence: drive-in (independent stop-on-X-threshold) → lift together (synchronized piston ramp) → Robot turns to face delivery direction (Robot2 frozen) → Robot2 turns (Robot frozen) → both drive straight to the delivery Y. Turning is staggered (not simultaneous) specifically to avoid the two chassis colliding — they start nose-to-nose with very little clearance.
- **Turn-to-exact-angle, not bearing-to-point**: earlier version computed a live bearing (`atan2`) to an absolute (x,y) target every tick, with continuous gentle steering correction during the drive. This was **rejected by the user** as unreliable — any small residual heading error meant the "straight" drive wasn't actually straight, so the two robots didn't stay predictably positioned relative to each other. Replaced with: turn to an *exact known* absolute yaw (analytically known from the fixed pickup headings, not derived from position), then drive with **equal wheel speeds and zero steering correction** once that heading is locked in. This fully fixed the correctness of the final position.
- **Stop-condition tolerance bug**: `drive_straight_step`'s stop condition was `abs(target_y - y) < stop_dist`. Since travel is monotonic (always approaching from below), this triggers the instant the robot enters that band from below — i.e. at `target_y - stop_dist`, not at `target_y`. Every prior "closest approach" (3.10 with stop_dist=0.15, then 3.206 with stop_dist=0.05) was landing systematically short by ~stop_dist, not from motor overshoot or the wrong target as first suspected. Fixed by checking `y >= target_y - small_margin` (reached-or-passed) instead of a symmetric distance band. See `feedback_small_scale_tight_tolerances` memory.
- **Speed ramping**: an instant full-speed (5.0) command from a standstill was what actually knocked the unattached payload object loose during the drive phase — not gradual path divergence, which was the first (wrong) hypothesis. Both `turn_to_yaw_step` and `drive_straight_step` now ramp speed linearly over `RAMP_STEPS=90` (~1.5s) from a standstill.
- **stdout buffering bug**: none of the script's `print()` output was reaching the redirected log file — Python fully block-buffers stdout when piped to a file (unlike a terminal), and the buffer was never filling/flushing while the process stayed alive. Fixed by launching with `PYTHONUNBUFFERED=1`.

### What worked
- Position-control (stiffness, not just damping) is the correct fix pattern for every "joint doesn't hold under load" symptom seen this session — piston, then later roll/pitch/yaw. Damping alone only ever resists *motion*, never a static load.
- Empirical, direct-measurement force tuning (drive it, measure whether the object actually lifts, adjust) was consistently more trustworthy than the static weight×gravity formula, which undersold real requirements every time it was tried.
- Exact-known-angle-then-straight-line was categorically more robust than continuous bearing-based correction for keeping two independently-controlled robots predictably positioned relative to each other.

### What did not work
- Continuous bearing-based steering correction during the drive phase (`atan2` toward a live target point each tick) — technically converges to the right point in isolation, but doesn't guarantee straight-line travel, which is what actually matters when a second robot's position needs to stay predictable relative to the first. Replaced with the exact-angle approach above.
- First hypothesis for the object falling off during carry: gradual heading-error divergence over the ~4m drive. Actually wrong — direct headless testing showed the drop happens almost immediately (within ~3 seconds of starting to move), which pointed to the real cause (instant full-speed command from a standstill), not slow drift.
- First hypothesis for the "wrong stop location": assumed rigid-coupling-mechanism absence (object sliding around) was the root cause of everything, including position. Wrong — position was a pure control-logic bug (the stop-condition tolerance issue above), unrelated to whether the object stays attached.

### Key decisions
- **No rigid coupling mechanism built yet** (the originally-planned "phase-one" fixed joint between plate and object). The tolerance/speed-ramp/exact-angle fixes above turned out to be sufficient to keep the object on the plates through a full pickup→turn→carry→arrive cycle via careful motion control alone, so building an explicit joint attachment was deprioritized. Still likely needed for a more general case (e.g. turning en route, not just at fixed points, or a bumpier path).
- **Piston/ball-joint maxForce values are testing-phase, not hardware-accurate** — deliberately raised past real actuator specs (same pattern as the earlier travel-limit override) so mechanism behavior can be tuned/observed without being bottlenecked by real hardware force ratings.

### Dependencies
- Robot's actual joint-offset and collision-geometry fixes live in `gitignore/paper_robot_centered_actuator_v6/.../base.usda` and `.../Physics/physics.usda` — **excluded from git** via `.gitignore`'s `gitignore/` rule. Not version-controlled anywhere. If this directory is ever cleaned up or the repo cloned fresh, these fixes are lost.
- `isaac/pickup_demo.py` and `isaac/two_robot_pickup_demo.py` both open `isaac/test/*.usd` scene files directly (`shelves1_with_collision.usd` and `two_robot_two_shelf.usd` respectively) — those files carry the shelf/payload/spawn-position state described in `isaac_environment.md`.

### [WORTH EXPLORING]
- Whether the current motion-control-only approach (no rigid attachment) holds up for more complex paths (turning mid-route, obstacles, uneven ground) or whether it was only robust because the test case was a single clean 90° turn plus a straight line.
- The residual 6.1cm gap between chassis top and platform minimum reach (from the Session 8 early joint-offset fix) is still dominated by the actuator's own fixed 0.1m chassis mount height — not revisited this session.

## Session 9 — NeuroWare (2026-08-12)

### Current state
Working: full 4-object delivery (`run_full_delivery()` in `two_robot_pickup_demo.py`) — Robot solo-carries Cube then Cylinder, Robot2 solo-carries Cube_02, both cooperate on Cube_01, each robot's plate held at a fixed world orientation the whole run via continuous yaw compensation. Working: a depth camera per robot producing real, correct distance data. Broken/unfinished: the reactive obstacle-detour built on top of that sensing gets physically stuck against a real obstacle with no recovery path — confirmed live, not yet fixed.

### What was done
- **Unload/lower step added** to the two-robot cooperative sequence: captured `piston_rest`/`piston_rest2` (the pre-lift piston position) before the lift ramp, then after the carry, ramp both pistons back down to that same value and print the object's Z before/after to confirm it actually settled rather than staying suspended.
- **Generalized navigation, replacing the old hardcoded shelf1->shelf2 route**:
  - `bearing_to(x, y, target_x, target_y)` — one-shot bearing calc (not re-derived live, matching the established exact-angle-then-straight preference).
  - `drive_to_point_step` — generalized from the old Y-only version. Stop condition is a **projection** onto the robot's actual current heading (`remaining = (target-pos)·(cos(yaw),sin(yaw))`), not raw Euclidean distance. This matters: raw distance is only monotonically decreasing if the straight line passes exactly through the target, and a locked-in heading is never exactly the intended bearing (measured settle drift was ~0.2°) — over a several-meter drive that produces a perpendicular miss bigger than the stop tolerance, so distance bottoms out just above threshold and starts climbing again as the robot drives past. Caught by headless smoke test: a leg missed its target by ~0.014m and then drove ~2m past it before exhausting its step budget. The projection is immune to any perpendicular miss since it only tracks progress along the line actually being driven.
  - `run_turn`/`run_drive`/`run_goto` — blocking wrappers combining the above into "turn to bearing, then drive." `run_turn`'s step budget was raised from 1500 to 3000: the original two-object version only ever turned ~90° (fixed pickup headings toward +Y), but generalized navigation between arbitrary objects can require turns close to 180° (e.g. one object's delivery heading back around to the next object's approach bearing is ~184°), which didn't reliably finish in 1500 steps — the turn silently ran out of budget mid-rotation with no warning message (unlike `run_drive`, which already had one) and the following drive then failed too. Both `run_turn` and `run_drive` now print a WARNING if they exhaust their step budget.
- **Multi-object task structure**: `solo_pickup_carry_lower(r, wj, pj, piston_start, obj_path, label, cam)` — full single-robot cycle (approach via `run_goto`, lift, carry via `run_goto`, lower), generalizing the single-robot logic from `pickup_demo.py` to take robot/joint handles and an object path as parameters instead of being hardcoded to one object. `run_full_delivery()` runs Robot through Cube then Cylinder, Robot2 through Cube_02, sequentially (not literally simultaneous — each robot fully finishes its own list before the next step), then both converge on Cube_01 for the existing cooperative straddle-lift logic (kept essentially unchanged, still uses fixed `TARGET_X`/`TARGET_X2` constants since Cube_01 is the only two-robot object). Real object masses/positions were queried directly from the running scene before designing this (see `isaac_environment.md`) rather than assumed.
- **Continuous yaw compensation**: `chassis_yaw0`/`chassis_yaw0_2` are now captured once, right after setup, and `apply_yaw_compensation` is called every tick via `sim_tick()` for the entire autonomous run — not just during the Cube_01 carry as before. This keeps the plate's world-frame orientation (long axis parallel to the shelves' long axis, required to slide under/out without jamming) correct at every pickup and delivery automatically, with no explicit re-alignment step needed between legs.
- **Depth camera per robot** (`isaacsim.sensors.camera.Camera`, one per robot, parented directly under each robot's `base_link` so it rides along with the chassis automatically):
  - Mount point derived from real chassis geometry in `base.usda` (not guessed): `chassis_visual` is a 0.3×0.15×0.04 box centered at local (0,0,0.064) → front edge at local x=0.15, top around z=0.084. Camera mounted just above that, at the front, facing local +X (confirmed as forward via wheel link positions: `front_left_wheel_link` at local x=+0.105, `rear_left_wheel_link` at x=-0.105).
  - **Camera orientation bug**: a hand-derived look-at quaternion assuming USD's usual `-Z-forward` convention pointed the camera at "completely the wrong area" (user's direct observation). Root cause, found by reading `camera.py` directly rather than guessing again: the `Camera` class's default `camera_axes="world"` convention (used by both `set_world_pose` and `set_local_pose`) is `+Z up, +X forward`, not USD's own convention. Fixed by rebuilding the look-at quaternion under the correct convention.
  - **Quaternion-extraction bug**: even after fixing the axis convention, a hand-rolled trace-based quaternion-from-rotation-matrix formula produced `NaN` for one specific camera orientation (a near-180° rotation from the matrix's reference frame — a known instability in that formula near trace ≈ -1). Fixed by using `scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation.from_matrix(...).as_quat()` instead of the hand-rolled formula.
  - **Near-clip bug**: USD cameras default to a `clippingRange` around (1, 1e6) — anything closer than 1m is invisible. The earlier isolated smoke tests never hit this (targets were 3-4m away), but this robot is ~0.3m and its nearest geometry (the ground right below/ahead) is well under 1m, so every pixel read `inf` by default. Confirmed directly (raw `distance_to_camera` array: 0/16384 finite) before concluding it wasn't a render-product/annotator-attachment problem. Fixed with `camera.set_clipping_range(near_distance=0.02, far_distance=50.0)`.
  - **Field of view**: default USD aperture/focal-length combo is a normal-lens still-camera FOV, about 24° horizontal — too narrow to notice anything off to the side. Widened to roughly 90° via `camera.set_focal_length(10.5)`, both for earlier warning and to make left-vs-right depth comparison meaningful (see detour direction below).
  - **Mount tilt**: first mounted tilted 30° down toward the floor (matching an earlier design discussion before any live testing). Live testing showed this only ever saw the floor a few inches ahead, missing everything at actual chassis height (the other robot, shelf structure) — changed to level (0° tilt, straight along local +X) so the horizon stays in view.
  - `min_depth(cam)` / `raw_depth_frame(cam)` — closest finite distance in view (or the full array), with a fallback from `cam.get_depth()` (which checks for an annotator key, `"distance_to_image_plane"`, that `add_distance_to_camera_to_frame()` never actually registers — a real mismatch inside the SDK's own deprecated `Camera` class, always logs a "not attached" warning regardless of success) to `cam.get_current_frame().get("distance_to_camera")`, which is the key that's actually populated and does carry real data.
- **Reactive obstacle detour**, scoped deliberately after discussion (options ranged from a plain stop, to a detour reflex, to full occupancy-grid path planning — the middle option was chosen): `run_drive`/`run_goto` accept an optional `cam`; if given, the drive stops early if `min_depth(cam) < OBSTACLE_THRESHOLD` (0.4m) and returns `"blocked"` instead of `"arrived"`/`"timeout"`. `run_goto` then turns toward whichever side `choose_detour_direction(cam)` reports more room (comparing the closest finite distance in the left half of the depth frame vs. the right half — only meaningful once the FOV was widened), drives a fixed 0.5m hop, and retries toward the **original** target (up to `MAX_DETOUR_ATTEMPTS=3`). `cam` is only ever passed on **carry** legs, never **approach** legs — on approach the object being approached would itself trip the obstacle check and the robot would stop short of what it's supposed to reach.
- **Live diagnostic tooling**, built in direct response to a hard-to-diagnose live failure (see below):
  - `carrying` dict (`{"Robot": obj_path_or_None, "Robot2": ...}`) — set right after a lift completes, cleared right after a lower completes, for both the solo cycle and the Cube_01 duo phase.
  - `print_trace()` — pose (position + yaw) for both robots and every known payload object (`/Cube`, `/Cube_01`, `/Cube_02`, `/Cylinder`) plus any spawned obstacles, called every `TRACE_INTERVAL` (600 ticks, ~10s) from `sim_tick()`. Tags an object with which robot's plate it's on, if any.
  - `spawn_obstacle(r, label)` — bound to keys `O` (ahead of Robot) / `I` (ahead of Robot2): drops a small `UsdGeom.Cube` 0.5m directly ahead of the given robot's current position/heading, with real `CollisionAPI` + `RigidBodyAPI` applied via script. Built specifically to replace ad-hoc manual GUI cube creation for obstacle testing, since a manually-created cube turned out to get silently auto-saved into the shared scene file (see `isaac_environment.md`) and pollute every subsequent "clean" test.
  - `identify_obstacle(cam)` — does a real PhysX raycast (`get_physx_scene_query_interface().raycast_closest(...)`) from the camera's actual world position/forward direction (computed via quaternion-rotating the local +X forward vector), returning the hit `rigidBody` path and distance. Built to settle, with direct ground truth, exactly what a depth-triggered obstacle stop is actually seeing, instead of reasoning about mount geometry on paper (which had already been wrong twice this session for the camera orientation itself).

### What worked
- Headless testing before live testing caught every navigation and camera bug this session (turn budget, stop-condition projection, camera axis convention, quaternion NaN, near-clip, narrow FOV) without needing the live GUI first — consistent with the pattern established last session.
- The projection-based stop condition and the wide-turn-budget fix were both confirmed via the exact same headless smoke-test harness used throughout the session (auto-converts the live script to headless, calls `run_full_delivery()` directly, prints world-coordinate diagnostics) — this harness itself is a reusable, low-cost verification tool at this point.
- Direct, ground-truth diagnostics were consistently the thing that actually resolved ambiguity this session: real object masses/positions queried from the running scene (not assumed) before designing the multi-object task split; the PhysX raycast identifying exactly what a "false" obstacle detection was actually hitting.

### What did not work
- **Camera orientation, twice**: first attempt had no orientation set at all (viewport showed nothing when switched to that camera). Second attempt assumed USD's `-Z-forward` convention and aimed exactly along the target cube's top face (grazing the edge, not the body — center pixel still read `inf`). Root cause of "pointing at completely the wrong area" was the `Camera` class's actual default convention being `+Z up, +X forward`, confirmed only by reading `camera.py` source directly.
- **Hand-rolled quaternion-from-matrix conversion**: produced `NaN` for a near-180° rotation case (camera #0 in a 6-camera ring test, pointed back along -X) — a known numerical instability in that formula near trace ≈ -1. Replaced with `scipy.spatial.transform.Rotation.from_matrix(...)`.
- **A synthetic teleport-based repro** (jumping a robot directly to a suspect world pose via `set_world_poses` to fast-forward past a slow leg) used the wrong Z height (copied from a payload object's resting height instead of the chassis's own), wedging the chassis into geometry and producing a misleadingly consistent "frozen, can't turn" symptom that had nothing to do with the real bug being investigated. Abandoned in favor of instrumenting the real script directly.
- **Fixed-direction, single-hop obstacle detour**: confirmed live to get physically stuck. Sequence from the actual log: obstacle detected at 0.388m → turn 60° → hop 0.5m → still only 0.129m clear → turn another (fixed-direction) 60°-ish → chassis physically wedged, yaw genuinely not changing for the entire 3000-step turn budget, never recovers for the rest of the run (every subsequent leg shows the same stall pattern). The script has no hard failure check anywhere in this path, so it still printed "all 4 objects delivered" at the end despite this — that message turned out to mean "the script reached that line," not "delivery actually succeeded." Root cause partially addressed since (turn direction now compares left/right depth instead of always turning the same way — see `choose_detour_direction`), but the clearance-per-hop and no-recovery-once-stuck issues are still open.
- **A live "self-occlusion" hypothesis was wrong, but not obviously so**: a detour reliably triggered on the Cube carry leg even in a from-scratch headless run with zero obstacles placed by anyone, which looked exactly like the camera seeing its own carried cargo. The actual cause (confirmed via `identify_obstacle`'s raycast returning `{'hit': False}` at that exact camera position/direction, followed by a direct bounding-box scan of the whole stage) was a manually-created GUI cube (`/Cube_03`, no collision) that had been silently auto-saved into `two_robot_two_shelf.usd` itself during an earlier live session, so it was present in every subsequent "clean" launch, headless or not. See `isaac_environment.md` for the file-level detail. The user's own live intuition ("the camera shouldn't be able to see the object above it") was correct and pointed at the real geometry (the elevated carried object's collision box sits well above the level camera's line of sight, confirmed by height math) well before the raycast tooling existed to prove it definitively.

### Key decisions
- **Obstacle-avoidance scope, decided explicitly with the user**: reactive stop-and-detour, not full occupancy-grid path planning. The grid/A* option was raised and explicitly declined in favor of extending the existing generalized point-to-point navigation instead.
- **`cam` only ever passed on carry legs, never approach legs** — a structural choice baked into `solo_pickup_carry_lower`'s call sites, not just a threshold tuning question. Getting this wrong (checking on approach too) would make every pickup attempt trigger its own target as an obstacle.
- **Detour direction chosen by depth comparison, not fixed** — direct response to the live stuck-robot failure; the fixed-direction version is confirmed strictly worse (turned further into the obstacle on its second attempt: 0.388m → 0.129m, closer, not clearer).
- **Scripted obstacle spawning (`O`/`I` keys) over manual GUI creation** — decided after the stray-cube discovery specifically so future obstacle tests don't risk silently polluting the shared scene file again.

### Dependencies
- Robot's actual joint-offset and collision-geometry fixes still live in `gitignore/paper_robot_centered_actuator_v6/.../base.usda` and `.../Physics/physics.usda` — excluded from git, not version-controlled anywhere (carried over from Session 8, unchanged this session). The depth camera's mount-geometry constants (`CAMERA_MOUNT_POS`, chassis dimensions) were read from this same un-version-controlled file.
- `two_robot_pickup_demo.py` opens `isaac/test/two_robot_two_shelf.usd` directly — that file currently contains a stray manually-created object (`/Cube_03`) not represented anywhere in this script; see `isaac_environment.md`.
- The headless smoke-test harness (auto-generated from the live script by flipping `headless` and replacing the keyboard/manual-control tail with a direct `run_full_delivery()` call) is not itself a committed file — it's regenerated fresh each time from the real script via a small inline Python string-replace, so it can never drift out of sync with the real script by accident.

### [POTENTIAL FIX]
- Obstacle-detour clearance: increase `DETOUR_DISTANCE` (currently 0.5m) and/or make it scale with how close the obstacle actually was, rather than a fixed hop — the live failure's second hop was still only 0.129m clear, well under a robot chassis width.
- Stuck-recovery: `run_turn`'s existing `STALL WARNING` detection (tick-to-tick yaw not changing) already fires in this exact failure case — it just isn't wired to *do* anything yet. A natural next step is triggering a different recovery maneuver (e.g. reverse a short distance, then re-attempt the detour turn) specifically when a stall is detected during a detour turn, rather than only printing a warning and continuing to burn through the step budget.

### [WORTH EXPLORING]
- Whether the current motion-control-only approach (still no rigid coupling between plate and object) continues to hold up now that routes involve real turns mid-route (every solo return-to-shelf1 leg) and not just the original single clean pickup->turn->carry->arrive case — no object has visibly fallen off yet this session, but it hasn't been stress-tested against the fixed obstacle-detour maneuvers either.
- `get_depth()`'s internal key mismatch (checks for `"distance_to_image_plane"`, which `add_distance_to_camera_to_frame()` never registers) always logs a spurious "not attached" warning on every call, on every tick, in this deprecated SDK path — currently just noise (worked around via the `get_current_frame()` fallback), but worth being aware it's a real bug in the SDK's own deprecated `isaacsim.sensors.camera.Camera`, not a sign of anything wrong locally.

## Session 11 — NeuroWare (2026-08-13)

### Current state
Two separate, real wheel-control bugs behind "the robot randomly refuses to turn" are found and fixed, both verified via direct `get_dof_velocities` readback rather than inferred from behavior. A from-scratch headless run of the real (unmodified) script now completes a carry-leg turn cleanly (`final_yaw_err_deg=0.009`, `duration_ticks=305`) where it previously stalled every single time. Running against the full shelf scene (corner legs present) still stops short of a full delivery — but now for a confirmed real reason (a genuine leg collision, raycast-verified), not a phantom one. A third, unrelated-looking obstacle-distance false trip during drives is still open, not yet investigated.

### What was done
- **Bug #1 — yaw-compensation joint destabilizing the wheel drives during turns.** Isolated in a fully bare scene (`_diag_wheel_index.py`: one robot, ground plane, nothing else — no shelf, no cargo, no second robot) by commanding each wheel individually, then the exact `[left,right,left,right]` turn pattern `run_turn` actually uses, and reading back actual vs. commanded velocity every tick.
  - All 4 wheel joints and their DOF-index mapping (`get_dof_indices` vs. the articulation's native `dof_names` order) were confirmed correct and symmetric — ruled out first since an index swap was the cheapest explanation to check.
  - Bare wheel driving (all 4 equal, and the turn pattern with no yaw-compensation active) tracked cleanly and symmetrically in both directions — the raw wheel/chassis physics is fine on its own.
  - Turning **with** `apply_yaw_compensation` active (driving the ball-yaw joint to counter-rotate the plate against the chassis every tick, exactly as every real turn does) reliably blew up wheel-velocity readbacks to 9-12x the commanded target with wrong signs on two wheels — but only in one rotation direction; the opposite direction stayed clean with the same yaw-compensation active. Fully deterministic, reproduced twice with identical numbers.
  - **[POTENTIAL FIX attempted, did not work]**: swept the yaw joint's stiffness/damping from the original (5000, 200) down through (2000,200), (1000,200), (500,150), (200,100), to pure damping (0,200) — with a corrected reset (actually teleporting the robot back to its exact spawn pose + zeroing all joint velocities between trials, since the first sweep attempt only zeroed wheel velocity and silently inherited momentum from the previous trial, producing meaningless comparisons). The instability was present at **every** gain level tested, including zero stiffness. Gain tuning is not a viable fix for this.

### What was done

**DWB → RPP → full custom controller, in that order, each step forced by live evidence:**
- Confirmed with the user that DWB's blended-arc motion (Session 12) was a real problem, not a preference — skid-steer chassis track pure rotation and pure straight-line driving far more predictably than any blended arc (established this session: continuous arcing requires every wheel slipping by a continuously-varying amount, the least predictable case for this drivetrain). Switched `nav2_params_robot1.yaml`'s `FollowPath` to `nav2_regulated_pure_pursuit_controller::RegulatedPurePursuitController` with `use_rotate_to_heading: True`. Noted and confirmed with the user: RPP hard-disables `allow_reversing` whenever `use_rotate_to_heading` is true (mutually exclusive in the plugin itself, not a config choice) — user explicitly prioritized no-arcing over reverse-while-driving.
- **RPP rotate-to-heading stall, root-caused live:** commanded rotation pinned near a small, non-converging value (~0.071 rad/s) regardless of a much larger configured target (1.0 rad/s). Isolated by reading RPP's actual behavior: `rotateToHeading()` computes `target = current_achieved_velocity ± (max_angular_accel * dt)` every control cycle, using real odom-fed velocity as the base, not the previously-commanded value or a persisted ramp state. Since the chassis wasn't yet accelerating, every cycle rebuilt the same small step from the same near-zero base — a genuine feedback deadlock, not a tuning-can't-reach-it problem. Fixed by raising `max_angular_accel` far enough that the *first* cycle's step already exceeds the target regardless of the stalled feedback base: went 1.5 (original, matched old DWB's `acc_lim_theta`) → 6.0 (broke the immediate stall but was still throttling every inter-waypoint turn since Nav2's grid waypoints are naturally 8-20deg apart, well under the old `rotate_to_heading_min_angle`-driven ramp range) → 25.0 (`accel*dt=1.25 > target(1.0)`, first cycle already clamps to full target). Matching `velocity_smoother.max_accel/max_decel` angular terms raised the same way, since a lower cap there would have re-throttled RPP's bigger step before it ever reached the wheels.
- **Wheel-drive red herring, fully chased and reverted:** while diagnosing the above, a bigger `max_angular_accel` initially produced *worse* symptoms — commanded value oscillating wildly (0.014→0.5→0.13→0.5 rad/s tick to tick) and ~0.4m of real, un-commanded lateral drift despite `linear.x` staying `0.0` the whole time (confirmed via direct `/cmd_vel_nav` + `/odom` sampling). Hypothesized the wheel joint's damping gain (`5000`, set in `setup_robot()`) was too high relative to this wheel's real (tiny) rotational inertia, causing torque to overshoot/flip sign every physics substep (a classic explicit-integrator instability when gain is disproportionate to inertia). Tested a torque cap (`r.set_dof_max_efforts(2.0, ...)`, later swept live 5/10/20/50 N·m via a new remote-control file) — every value from 2 to 50 N·m produced *zero* measurable rotation, worse than uncapped, ruling out "not enough torque." Also tried sweeping damping down (5000→5.0 live) — also zero response, ruling out "damping too high" too. Root cause found by checking process state directly: a leftover Nav2 stack from an earlier `max_angular_accel=6.0` test had never actually been killed (only its active goal was cancelled) — `velocity_smoother` was still alive and continuously publishing to `/cmd_vel`, double-publishing against every subsequent raw test and corrupting every measurement taken during the torque/damping investigation. Once genuinely killed, a clean raw `/cmd_vel` test at the *original* `damping=5000`, no torque cap, converged smoothly to a real, stable `~0.39 rad/s` from a held `0.5 rad/s` command over ~4-5s — confirming the original wheel config was fine all along. Reverted the torque cap entirely and restored `damping=5000` as the default (still exposed via the same live-sweep file for future use). Kept one legitimate finding from this detour: PhysX was putting the idle articulation to sleep after several minutes at rest (confirmed via the existence of `set_sleep_thresholds()` in the Articulation API, and the fact that the exact same clean config that worked immediately after boot stopped responding after minutes of idling) — added `r.set_sleep_thresholds(0.0)` in `setup_robot()` so it can't happen again; this was not the fix for the accel-ramp deadlock, a separate real issue found along the way.
- **Full commit to zero-curvature, per explicit user decision:** even with RPP's rotate-to-heading fixed, driving still blends curvature into any correction under `rotate_to_heading_min_angle` (45deg) — user's reasoning: this project's world is small-scale/tight-tolerance now and will only get tighter (more objects, less open space) later, so *any* curvature is a liability, not an efficiency win, given this chassis already struggles to track combined rotate+translate motion cleanly (established above). Confirmed with the user this requires dropping Nav2's controller layer entirely (RPP has no "zero curvature always" mode — pure-pursuit's blended phase is structural to how it drives, not a tunable parameter), not just further RPP tuning. Presented three options (custom C++ Nav2 plugin / hybrid keep-planner-drop-controller / full hand-rolled no-Nav2) — user chose the hybrid: keep `planner_server`'s real costmap-based routing, replace only the execution layer.

**New file: `isaac/rotate_drive_controller.py`** — standalone `rclpy` node, launched via plain `python3` (confirmed this works fine outside Isaac's Kit process — the Session-12 segfault is specifically about rclpy *inside* Kit's Python 3.12, not rclpy in general; this node never touches Isaac Sim directly, only talks to the already-running bridge over `/odom`/`/scan`/`/cmd_vel`/`/goal_pose`).

### What was done

**Reactive check was omnidirectional, fixed to a forward cone** (`rotate_drive_controller.py`):
- `_scan_cb` used to take `min()` over the *entire* 360° synthesized scan (`msg.ranges`, no angular filtering at all). Confirmed live: this meant an obstacle already passed and now behind the chassis (shelf 1's own corner leg, right after the exit turn) kept tripping the reactive stop, since the check has no notion of "in my way" vs "somewhere nearby."
- Fixed: `_scan_cb` now walks `msg.ranges` with `angle_min`/`angle_increment` and only keeps samples within `FORWARD_HALF_ANGLE` (±90°, body-frame — rotates with the chassis, so during a TURN it's "ahead of wherever the nose currently points") of straight ahead.

**Proactive replanning, sensor-grounded per direct user correction** (`rotate_drive_controller.py`):
- First proposal (periodic blind replan on a timer) was explicitly rejected by the user in favor of grounding the trigger in real sensor/costmap evidence. Built instead: subscribed to `/global_costmap/costmap` (`nav_msgs/OccupancyGrid`) — confirmed live via `ros2 topic info` against the running `planner_server` that this is already published (`publish_frequency: 1.0`, `always_send_full_costmap: True`, both already set in `nav2_params_robot1.yaml`).
- `_lookahead_check()`: samples the *remaining* path (current position through each remaining waypoint) every `LOOKAHEAD_SAMPLE_SPACING`=0.15m (reuses the same scale `simplify_path` already collapses the planner's raw grid path to), converts each sample to a grid cell via the grid's own `info.origin`/`info.resolution` (assumes an axis-aligned rolling-window costmap — true for Nav2's standard implementation), and checks cost against `LOOKAHEAD_LETHAL_COST`=99 (confirmed via live `ros2 topic echo`: nav2_costmap_2d's `OccupancyGrid` wire encoding maps `LETHAL_OBSTACLE`→100, `INSCRIBED_INFLATED_OBSTACLE`→99, everything else (soft inflation gradient) scaled below that — so this threshold means "genuinely too close," not "any nonzero preference cost," which would have fired constantly in this tight scene).
- Debounced across `LOOKAHEAD_DEBOUNCE_COUNT`=2 consecutive costmap messages before triggering a replan, to filter a single noisy reading (mirrors the existing `CAMERA_OVERLAP_RECONCILE_TOL`/`_median_smooth` discipline in `nav2_bridge_robot1.py`).
- Reuses the existing `_request_plan(self.goal_xy)` call — no new planning path. Deliberately does NOT clear `self.state`/`self.waypoints` when triggering (unlike the reactive obstacle-trip path), so `_plan_result`'s existing dedup-guard (only replaces the route if the new plan's first waypoint differs meaningfully from the current one) applies automatically — a same-target replan doesn't restart the drive ramp.
- **Real bug caught before it shipped**: the proactive check initially fired regardless of `direct_mode`/`obstacle_check_enabled`, meaning it could have silently overwritten a `direct_mode` leg's single-waypoint route (e.g. the final shelf-2 entry, deliberately not planner-routed to avoid clipping rail structure) with a multi-waypoint planner route. Caught in the very first test run's log (proactive-replan lines appearing during a `carry-clear-direct` leg) before it caused visible harm. **Per-leg planner clearance sync** (`rotate_drive_controller.py`):
- `_request_plan` now syncs `planner_server`'s `global_costmap.robot_radius` to `_required_clearance()` (the same `OBSTACLE_STOP_RANGE`/`CARRY_OBSTACLE_STOP_RANGE` the reactive check already uses — factored into one shared method specifically so the two can never drift onto different numbers) before sending the plan goal. **"Correction, not compensation" for carrying orientation** — the actual mechanism, split across both processes:
- `nav2_bridge_robot1.py`: `apply_yaw_compensation` used to run unconditionally every tick (not even gated on whether anything was being carried). Added `YAW_COMPENSATION_CONTROL_FILE` (`/tmp/robot1_yaw_compensation`, `on`/`off`), gates the call. Default stays "on" so nothing else that depends on it is affected.
- `rotate_drive_controller.py`: added `_start_leg(..., absolute_yaw=None)` — when given, sets `self.leg_target_yaw` directly instead of computing a bearing-to-a-point via `atan2`. - `shelf_transfer_task.py`: subscribes to `/odom` directly (new — previously only used the bridge's polled query mechanism for position, not live orientation) to capture `shelf1_exit_yaw` right when the exit-from-shelf-1 leg completes. **Precision-stop tuning** (`rotate_drive_controller.py`):

### Key decisions
- Suspending yaw compensation during the detour and correcting once, deliberately, at a stationary point — rather than trying to make the continuous real-time compensation track faster (e.g. only via more torque) — per direct user design discussion: decouples "get roughly there" (free chassis rotation, no fighting) from "get precisely into the required final configuration" (unhurried, no time pressure). This is the actual structural fix; the `maxForce` raise is a secondary, less-load-bearing change on top of it.
- Chose NOT to touch the shared `MIN_RAMP_OUT`/turn-speed tuning at all when the overshoot problem showed up, introducing a separate final-approach-only constant instead — avoids any risk to the already-proven turn/general-driving behavior for a fix that only needed to apply to one specific stop.
